# ADR-001: Randomisation, Missing Outcomes and Safety-Gated Decisions

**Status:** Accepted for synthetic prototype; not approved for production deployment.

**Context.** The project must show whether a workflow intervention can be evaluated causally while handling missing feedback. It must never place high-risk reports in a fast-moving experiment. The current portfolio contains a risk taxonomy, fixed severity floors and human-review rules.

**Decision.** Assign a stable 50/50 arm via SHA-256 of experiment salt and unit ID, after the eligibility guard. Maintain an explicit `excluded` arm; don't treat protected records as controls. Record the outcome observation indicator and simulated known propensity. Compare observed-only, self-normalised IPW and baseline-adjusted IPW+CUPED estimates. Fail closed on protected exposures, changed human review, inadequate sample size and insufficient statistical evidence. Never issue a production launch approval.

**Alternatives considered.** Sequential adaptive randomisation could increase exposure to promising policies but complicates inference and creates safety risk; defer it. A simple before/after comparison is cheaper but cannot isolate time-varying confounders. Dropping missing outcomes can introduce selection bias. Automatically routing sensitive cases using a confidence threshold alone was rejected because the asymmetry of harmful mistakes is high.

**Consequences.** Stable reproducibility, inspectability and tests are improved. This is only an offline simulation: the assumed missingness mechanism may not match reality, and the model evaluation does not constitute safety validation. The analytical review gate is a demo of governance, not a real rollout decision.

**Follow-up evidence before production:** experiment-registration document, safety committee review, experimental power design, privacy/security review, data quality controls, independent propensity validation, cohort monitoring, sequential testing and actual business outcome measurement.
